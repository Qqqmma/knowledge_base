# Unit 7 - Present perfect 1
## Conspect
### Present perfect simple
The present perfect simple is:
- have/has + past participle (often ends in -ed, but many verbs are *irregular*)

When we say 'something has happened', this is usualy new information:
- Ow! **I've cut** my finger.
- The road is closed. **There's been** an accident (= There has been)
- Police **have arested** two men in connection with the robbery.
When we use present perfect, there is connection with *now*. The action in the past has results *now*.
- Tom **has lost** his key. (= he doesn't have in *now*)
- He told me his name, but **I've forgotten** it. (= I can't remember it *now*)
- Sally is still here. She **hasn't gone** out. (= she is here *now*)
- I can't find my dog. **Have you seen** it? (do you know where it is *now*)
Compare **gone** (to) and **been** (to).
- James is on holiday. He **has gone to** Italy. (= he is there now or on his way there)
- Amy is back home now. She **has been to** Italy. (= she has now come back)

You can use present perfect with **just**, **already** and **yet**:
- Just = a short time ago
	- 'Are you hungry?' - 'No, I **have just had** lunch.'
	- Hello. **Have you** just **arrived**?
- Already = sooner then expected:
	- Don't forget to pay the bill. - I've already paid it.
	- What time is Mark leaving? - He's already left.
- Yet = until now. We use yet to show that we are expecting something to happen. We use **yet** in questions and negative sentences:
	- Has it stopped raining yet?
	- I've written the email, but I haven't sent it yet.

We also can use past simple (went, did, had etc.):
- Ben isn't here. He's gone out. *or* He went out.
- 'Are you hungry?' - 'No, **I've just had** lunch.' *or* 'No, I just **had** lunch.'
